The problem

Testing a voice bot today means people making test calls

Slow

A tester gets through a handful of calls an hour.

Inconsistent

Two testers grade the same call differently.

Narrow

Real callers have accents, noise and odd requests. Test calls rarely do.

Risky

Every change to the bot’s script can quietly break something that worked.

The idea

Three AI roles that a contact centre already understands

Mystery shopper

Plays a caller with a personality and a goal, like “annoyed customer returning a cracked blender”, and phones the bot.

QA supervisor

Listens back and fills in a scorecard: did the caller get what they needed, were the rules followed, did it sound right on the phone?

Coach

Reads the worst calls and rewrites the bot’s instructions to fix the cause, then the calls are run again.

Step 1 · Connect

Does it find the voice agent from the app ID?

Yes, automatically

  • You type three things: Google Cloud project, region, app ID
  • It connects and lists every agent inside that app
  • It reads the chosen agent’s current instructions and shows them on screen
  • It then calls that agent through Google’s own testing channel

You still decide

  • Which agent to test, picked from a dropdown, since an app can hold several
  • What the test calls are about. AI can draft them from a PRD, but a business owner reviews them
  • Whether an improvement is applied

Each test call is a short description: who is calling, what they want, what they know, and what success looks like. No scripts to write.

Step 2 · A test call

From a five-line brief to a real phone conversation

The brief

Caller: elderly, speaks slowly

Wants: status of order 51870

Twist: says 51807 first, then corrects

Success: bot uses the corrected number and reads it back

Text mode

Caller and bot exchange typed lines. Takes seconds. Good for checking logic after every change.

Audio mode

The caller’s lines are spoken aloud in a chosen accent and pace, with optional car or street noise, and streamed to the bot like a real call. Every turn is recorded so you can listen back.

Step 3 · Score

Every call gets the same scorecard

Question on the scorecardWeight
Did the caller get what they called for?45%
Were the house rules followed?20%
Did it stick to real facts, nothing made up?15%
Did it sound right on the phone?15%
Was the tone polite and natural?5%

“Right on the phone” means

  • Short turns, not long speeches
  • One question at a time
  • No web links or formatting read aloud
  • No awkward silence before answering

Audio mode adds

  • Did the bot hear correctly?
  • Seconds of dead air, measured

Steps 4 and 5 · Coach and re-test

A change is kept only when it wins on calls it has never seen

Practice calls

The coach studies the failures here and writes new instructions, for example “read the order number back before looking it up”.

Exam calls

Held back and never shown to the coach. If scores drop here, the change only memorised the practice calls, so it is rejected.

Ground rules

The coach cannot remove the bot’s tools or its standard structure. A rewrite that tries is thrown out before testing.

Each round shows a chart of scores, what changed and why, a side-by-side of old and new instructions, and every call recording.

Step 6 · Approve

Nothing reaches customers without a person saying yes

Coaching runs on a copy of the bot, never the live one

The original instructions are saved first and put back after every run

A reviewer clicks Apply on the version they want, or Restore original

Test calls use pretend back-end systems, so no real orders or accounts are touched

Where to use it

Five moments in a voice bot’s life

MomentWhat the tool answers
Before go-liveIs it good enough to put in front of customers? A baseline score per call type.
After every changeDid fixing one thing break another? Re-run the full test pack in minutes.
New region or languageDoes it cope with Indian, British or Australian English, fast talkers, noisy lines?
After complaintsTurn a complaint into a test call, then let the coach work on it until it passes.
Choosing a model or vendorSame test pack, two set-ups, one scorecard: a like-for-like comparison.
